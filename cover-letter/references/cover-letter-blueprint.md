# Cover letter blueprint — gate, structure, and honesty rules

A cover letter is short, so every line has to earn its place. This is how to gate on fit, structure the letter, and keep it truthful.

## The fit gate (run before writing)

Classify the candidate against the posting's hard requirements and top priorities:

| Verdict | Test | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Fit** | Meets the hard gates and most top priorities | Write a confident letter. |
| **Stretch** | Meets the gates, misses some priorities but has real adjacent proof | Write, led by drive + closest proof; address the main gap honestly. |
| **Don't bother (yet)** | Fails a hard gate (degree/authorization/language/seniority) or hits none of the top priorities | **Stop. Tell the candidate.** Name the gaps; hand to `career-advisor` (skills) or `job-matcher` (better-fit roles). Write only if they insist, labelled an honest long shot. |

Being the honest friend here is the whole point — a beautiful letter for a role someone will be auto-filtered from is a disservice.

## Private / unlisted work

The strongest proof is sometimes in a private repo the employer can't see. When gathering evidence:
- **Ask, don't assume.** "You have private work I can't see — do you want to share it?" Never access anything without consent.
- If access is given (e.g. the candidate's own `gh` login) and a private project is genuinely strong for this role, **suggest making it public** — an employer can't verify a repo they can't open, so a public version is worth far more in an application.
- If it can't be public, reference it truthfully and modestly ("available on request" / describe it without a link) rather than dropping real work entirely — but prefer public, verifiable proof in the letter.

## Structure (one page, ~250–350 words)

1. **Hook (2–3 sentences)** — who the candidate is in one line + the single most relevant, specific reason they're a strong match for *this* role. Skip "I am writing to apply for…".
2. **Proof (1–2 short paragraphs)** — the 2–3 strongest real wins mapped directly to the posting's top priorities. Show, with specifics and outcomes; don't list adjectives.
3. **Company fit (2–3 sentences)** — a concrete, researched reason for this company (product, mission, stack, values). No generic flattery ("your prestigious company").
4. **Close (2 sentences)** — reaffirm interest, a light call to action (glad to talk / share more), thanks. Sign off simply.

## Honesty rules

- Every claim traces to the candidate's real evidence (CV, verified GitHub, evidence map, or something they confirmed). If a detail would strengthen the letter but no source supports it, leave a `[confirm: …]` placeholder — never invent it.
- Match enthusiasm to reality — motivation can be genuine even when experience is thin, but don't manufacture a "lifelong passion" that isn't there.
- Mirror the posting's real title and the skills the candidate actually has; don't keyword-stuff terms they can't back up.
- Address gaps with transferable proof or fast-ramp evidence, never by claiming the missing skill.

## Clichés to cut (unless backed by a concrete example)

"I am writing to express my interest" · "team player" · "hard worker" · "detail-oriented" · "think outside the box" · "fast-paced environment" · "wear many hats" · "passionate about leveraging synergies". Replace each with a specific thing the candidate actually did.
