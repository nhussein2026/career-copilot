# Outreach playbook — the five message types

Every message: true relationship register, one real hook, one small ask, easy to act on. Length caps are hard caps — outreach dies by paragraph three.

## 1. Referral request (the highest-converting message — spend the most care here)

**To**: someone at the target company. Register by truth: former colleague ≠ alum-you-never-met ≠ stranger.
**Structure** (email/DM, ≤120 words + the forwardable block):
1. True context in one line ("we worked at X" / "fellow <school> alum — we haven't met" / "found you via the team page").
2. The situation, plainly: applying to <role>, and why it's a genuine fit in one specific sentence (from real evidence).
3. The ask with an out: "If you're comfortable, could you refer me or forward this to the hiring manager? If not, no problem at all."
4. **The forwardable block** — do the work for them: the posting link + a 2-line blurb (who the candidate is + the one most relevant proof point) they can paste onward.

Rules: never ask a stranger to vouch for quality — ask them to *forward*, not to *endorse*. One referral ask per company at a time. If they don't reply in a week, ask nobody else at the same company for another week (parallel asks that collide embarrass everyone).

## 2. Cold note — recruiter or hiring manager

**Connection note** (LinkedIn, ≤300 chars): who + the role + one credibility fact + soft ask. Example shape: "Hi <name> — backend dev, 3 yrs Node/Postgres, just applied to your <role>. My messaging-platform project matches the posting closely. Happy to share more if useful." No "Dear Sir", no life story, no flattery.
**InMail/DM/email** (one short paragraph, ≤120 words): same skeleton with one extra proof sentence and a link (portfolio or the exact repo — verified working).
Rules: hiring manager > recruiter when identifiable; reference the *specific* posting (recruiters juggle dozens); "any openings?" with no target role is noise — refuse politely and target first (job-matcher's ground).

## 3. Follow-up after applying

**When**: 7 business days after applying, no response. **Quota: one.**
**Email/DM, ≤80 words**: restate role + date applied (they shouldn't have to search), one line of added value if real news exists (shipped something relevant, new cert) or the strongest proof point restated in fresh words, reaffirm interest, done. Subject: "Following up — <Role> application (<Name>)".
Never guilt ("I know you're busy but…" / "I haven't heard back…" as a complaint). It's a signal of continued interest, not an invoice.

## 4. Thank-you after an interview

**When**: same day or next morning. Always worth sending; almost nobody's is specific.
**Email, ≤100 words**: thanks with the interviewer's name → **one specific moment from the conversation** (the question that made them think, the architecture discussed, the roadmap detail) → one line reinforcing fit *based on what was discussed* → looking forward to next steps.
The specific moment is the whole message — a thank-you that could have been written before the interview is worse than none. If the candidate can't recall one, ask them for it; don't invent one.

## 5. Status check after silence

**When**: stated timeline + 3 business days; no timeline stated → 7 business days after the interview. **Quota: one per stage.**
**Email, ≤60 words**: still interested, checking on timeline for <role>, understand these things take time, thanks. Zero pressure, zero guilt.
After the one check: the tracker marks the row and the candidate moves energy elsewhere — chasing harder doesn't reopen doors, and the playbook says so out loud.

## Lines never to cross (all types)

- No fake familiarity ("Hope you're doing great!" to a stranger; "reconnecting" with someone never met).
- No invented urgency or leverage ("other offers are moving fast" — unless literally true; that conversation belongs to offer-advisor anyway).
- No mass-blast tone: if swapping the recipient's name breaks nothing, the message isn't personalized enough to send.
- No trashing a current/former employer, ever, in any message.
- Respect the quotas above even when the candidate pushes — one nudge per stage; more converts nothing and marks the name.
