# LinkedIn rebuild playbook

How to rewrite each section so the whole profile pulls toward one goal. Every rewrite stays truthful to the candidate's real experience — you are re-angling and sharpening, never inventing.

## Share guide (give this if you don't have the profile)

LinkedIn returns HTTP 999 to bots, so it can't be fetched automatically. Ask the candidate to:
1. Open their profile → **More** (below the headline) → **Save to PDF** → attach it; **or**
2. Copy-paste each section as text: Headline, About, every Experience entry (title, company, dates, bullets), Skills, Education, Licenses & Certifications, Featured.

Don't rewrite a section you haven't seen — guessing produces confident nonsense.

## The one-goal rule

Before rewriting anything, lock the single target role. Judge every line by: *does this move the reader toward seeing me as a strong candidate for that role?* Cut or re-angle anything that doesn't. A profile optimized for two goals is optimized for none.

## Headline (2 options, ≤220 chars)
- Formula: `Target Role | Specialization / stack | value or proof`. Put the most-searched keyword (the target title) in the first ~40 chars — it's what shows on mobile and in search.
- No buzzwords ("ninja", "guru"). Real language only.
- **Headline claims, profile proves.** State the differentiator by *category* ("Open-source contributor") — don't cram a specific proper noun or metric into the headline (not "Open-source contributor (livecodes)", not "500+ users"); the About and Featured sections carry the specifics.
- Give two options so the candidate can pick their voice.

## About (full rewrite)
- **First 2–3 lines are everything** — they show before "…see more". Hook with who you are + what you do + the target — general, no specific metrics or project names (that's the headline/summary rule; keep the hook at claim level).
- First person, concrete, in the candidate's voice — not a third-person bio, not a copy of the resume summary.
- The later paragraphs are the proof section — here it's right to name real projects and contributions. **Round metrics** ("1,400+ stars", not "1,465") so they stay honest as they drift, and don't repeat the exact same numbers the Featured/Experience items already show.
- Weave in the target's hard skills as natural keywords (search matches plain words), without stuffing.
- End with a light call to action (what you're open to, how to reach you).

## Experience (each role)
- One-line scope/context (team, product, what it does) + 2–4 impact bullets: action + what + measurable outcome.
- Angle every bullet toward the target role; surface the relevant work, de-emphasize the rest.
- Must match the resume/dossier exactly on title, dates, and scope — recruiters cross-check.

## Skills (+ pin top 3)
- Set the full list to the skills genuinely held that matter for the target; use the target job descriptions' exact terms (string match matters in search).
- **Pin the top 3** (profile → Skills → reorder): these show first and are weighted heavily in recruiter search — make them the three most central to the target role.
- No "expert" framing the experience doesn't support.

## Featured
- Feature 2–4 proof items that back the target: a project's live link, a repo, a post, an article, or the portfolio. Verified links only.

## Profile hygiene (checklist to set)
- [ ] Professional photo; custom banner (not default gray)
- [ ] Custom vanity URL `linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname`
- [ ] Location set to the metro you want to be recruited in
- [ ] "Open to work" set (recruiter-visible at minimum) if job-seeking
- [ ] Contact info filled; Featured populated
- [ ] Name/title/dates identical to the resume and dossier

## Consistency pass (before delivering)
- No leftover language from an old career/goal anywhere on the profile.
- Headline, About, and Skills share the same core keywords.
- Nothing contradicts the resume/dossier.
- The whole thing reads as one person aimed at one goal.
