# Interview playbook — stories, gaps, walkthrough, mock

## The STAR spec (how each story is written)

Beats, not a script — the candidate should re-tell it naturally, not recite it:

- **Situation** — one line of context: where, when, what was at stake.
- **Task** — what *the candidate* owned (not the team's mission).
- **Action** — 2–4 beats of what they actually did; the decisions matter more than the tools.
- **Result** — the outcome with a real number where one exists (`[confirm: …]` where it should exist and doesn't).
- **Reflection** — one line: what they'd do differently or what it taught them (interviewers ask; having it ready turns a good story into a great one).

Quality bar: specific enough that a follow-up question ("what did the schema look like?", "who disagreed?") lands on real memory, not improvisation. If the candidate can't answer follow-ups on a story, it's not interview-ready — say so.

## Behavioral themes (map every story to at least one)

| Theme | The questions it answers |
|---|---|
| Impact / achievement | "Tell me about something you're proud of" · "Your biggest contribution?" |
| Hard problem | "Hardest bug/technical challenge?" · "A time you were stuck?" |
| Conflict / disagreement | "Disagreed with a teammate or manager?" · "Received hard feedback?" |
| Failure / lesson | "A time you failed?" · "A mistake and what you learned?" |
| Leadership / initiative | "Led something?" · "Started something nobody asked for?" |
| Ambiguity / pressure | "Unclear requirements?" · "Tight deadline — what did you cut?" |

Six strong stories with honest theme overlap beat twenty thin ones. The failure story is mandatory — everyone gets asked, few prepare it; the honest version (real failure, real lesson, no humble-brag) is a differentiator.

## Gap questions — the honest answer pattern

For every gap the posting or CV exposes (employment gap, short tenure, missing skill, pivot), prepare: **acknowledge → one sentence of true context → pivot to real proof.**

- *Missing skill:* "I haven't used Kafka in production — my streaming experience is with Redis pub/sub on [real project]; the concepts map, and here's how I'd close the gap in the first month." Never claim the missing skill.
- *Employment gap:* the true reason in one plain sentence (family, health, market, study — no apology spiral), then what they built/learned during it if anything real exists, then forward.
- *Short tenure:* the true reason without trashing the employer, then what they shipped in the time anyway.
- *Pivot:* the through-line that makes the pivot logical, told forward ("what I was moving toward"), not defensively.

Rule: nothing in a gap answer may contradict the resume, LinkedIn, or what a reference would say. Honest and brief beats elaborate every time.

## The walkthrough ("tell me about yourself" / "walk me through your resume")

~90 seconds, three beats, angled at *this* role:
1. **Now** — who they are professionally in one line (mirror the honest target-role language).
2. **The path** — 2–3 real waypoints chosen because they lead here; each with one concrete accomplishment, skipping everything that doesn't serve the thread.
3. **Why here, why now** — the true reason this role is the logical next step.

It's the interview's first impression and the only fully predictable question — worth rehearsing out loud twice.

## Questions to ask them (pick 5–8, tailor with real research)

- **The work**: "What does the team ship next quarter?" · "What's the hardest technical problem right now?"
- **Success**: "What does great look like in the first 90 days?" · "How is performance actually evaluated?"
- **The team**: "How is the team structured — who would I work with daily?" · "How do decisions get made when people disagree?"
- **Health signals** (listen carefully to these answers): "Why is the role open?" · "What's the biggest risk to the team's plans?"
- **Genuinely researched**: one question referencing something real — their engineering blog, a product launch, the stack from the posting. This one signals more than the other seven.

Skip questions answered on the website, and skip comp/benefits until there's an offer (that's `offer-advisor`'s ground).

## Mock interview protocol

1. One question at a time from the prep set — start with the walkthrough, then alternate behavioral and role-specific. Don't reveal the full list order; unpredictability is the training.
2. After each answer, feedback against this rubric, concretely:
   - **Specific?** Real details, or generic claims anyone could make?
   - **Their role clear?** "I" where it was them, "we" where it was the team — not all "we".
   - **Result stated?** Did the story land on an outcome, or trail off?
   - **Length**: 1.5–2.5 minutes for behavioral; shorter for factual. Flag rambling and one-line under-answers both.
   - **Honesty pressure-test**: ask one follow-up drilling into a detail; if the answer wobbles, the story needs work or trimming — better discovered here.
3. Re-ask the weakest answer at the end — improvement on the second pass is the point of the exercise.
